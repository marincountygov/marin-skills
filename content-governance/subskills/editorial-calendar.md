# Subskill: Editorial Calendar

## Purpose

Plan, schedule, coordinate, and maintain content across channels and time.

## Use when

Campaigns, seasonal content, news and announcements, public meetings or events, program launches, recurring updates, cross-channel communications, content maintenance cycles.

## Core rule

An editorial calendar is more than a publishing schedule — it connects audience need, channel, owner, review process, timing, lifecycle state (see `lifecycle-states.md`), and success measure.

## Calendar fields

Item title, content type, audience, user intent, channel, owner, draft/review/approval/publish/refresh/unpublish dates, dependencies, status, success measure.

## Status labels

Proposed, Planned, Drafting, In review, Approved, Scheduled, Published, Needs update, Archived, Retired.

## Channel planning

Identify the channel: website, intranet, email newsletter, social media, press release, printed material, partner toolkit, public meeting agenda, document library, service portal. Don't duplicate full content across channels without a clear need — route users to the single source of truth instead.

## Timing questions

What user need or event drives the timing? What must publish before this item? What reviews/approvals are required (see `approval-routing.md`)? Is the content seasonal, recurring, urgent, or evergreen? When does it go stale? What happens after the publish window ends?

## Campaign phasing

- **Pre-launch** — define audience and goals, prepare the source-of-truth page, draft supporting content, confirm metadata/tracking, schedule review and approval.
- **Launch** — publish core content, coordinate channel releases, monitor response, correct issues quickly.
- **Sustain** — refresh messaging, answer recurring questions, adjust channels based on performance.
- **Closeout** — unpublish/archive campaign-specific material, update evergreen content, record performance and lessons learned. Plan this before launch, not after.

## Required output

**Editorial calendar**: Date, Item, Type, Audience, Channel, Owner, Status, Dependency, Lifecycle action, Success measure.

**Maintenance calendar** (non-campaign governance): Content, Owner, Last reviewed, Next review, Review trigger, Required action, Status.

## Guardrail

Do not schedule publication without an owner. Do not schedule time-limited content without a refresh, archive, or unpublish date. For multi-channel content, identify the single source of truth. For recurring content, define the recurrence pattern and who confirms each cycle.
