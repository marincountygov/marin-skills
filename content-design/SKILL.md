# Content design

## Purpose

Answer, before content gets written: who is this for, what are they trying to do, what does this specific item need to accomplish, what kind of content is it, and where does it belong.

**This skill is the planning workflow. It is not where the underlying standard lives.** The audience/intent model, content-type definitions, page-purpose types, and taxonomy principles are canonical in [marin-digital-standards/content-design](https://github.com/marincountygov/marin-digital-standards/tree/main/content-design) (`principles.md`, `content-patterns.md`). Consult those for the concepts; use this skill and its subskills to apply them to a specific planning task.

## When to use

Creating or revising a content strategy for a page or set of pages · deciding whether content should exist, merge, split, or be redirected · defining audience, intent, purpose, type, or placement for a new or existing item · converting a vague content request into a clear, user-centered brief.

## When not to use

Not a substitute for plain-language editing (`plain-language-review`), accessibility review (`accessibility-review`), inclusive-language review (`inclusive-language-review`), or brand/visual review. This skill may identify that one of those reviews is needed; it doesn't perform them.

## Workflow

1. **Clarify the content object.** Is this a page, section, document, campaign, or something else? New, existing, migrated, or under review?
2. **Define audience and intent** — see `subskills/audience-intent.md`.
3. **Define the page or asset purpose** — see `subskills/page-purpose.md`.
4. **Classify content type** — see `subskills/content-types.md`.
5. **Determine placement and metadata** — see `subskills/placement-and-metadata.md`.
6. **Define success measures.** Prefer outcome measures (task completion, reduced support contacts, reduced confusion) over traffic volume alone — see `marin-digital-standards/content-design/principles.md`.

For ongoing ownership, review cadence, and scheduling once content is planned, hand off to `content-governance`. For evaluating content that already exists, use `content-audit`.

## Standard output: content strategy brief

Use for new content, major rewrites, migrations, or campaigns:

Title/working title · owner · primary and secondary audience · user intent · page/asset purpose · content type · recommended location · related content · metadata and taxonomy · success measures · risks, dependencies, or open questions.

## Decision rules

- Before recommending new content, confirm: there's a real user need, an identifiable audience, a responsible owner, a maintainable source of truth, and that similar content doesn't already meet the need. If any of these aren't true, recommend improving existing content instead.
- A page may support several related tasks but should have one primary purpose — if purposes compete, recommend splitting or restructuring rather than layering more content onto one page.
- Organize around what the user needs to do, not internal department structure, unless the request is genuinely for internal/governance content.

## Quality checklist

Before finalizing: the audience is specific, not "the public" · the intent is a task or question, not a topic · the purpose isn't just "provide information" · the content type matches the purpose · the recommended location is justified · duplicate or overlapping content has been identified · ownership is assigned or flagged as unresolved · success measures are meaningful.

## Style

Be direct and operational — recommendations, not abstract theory. Use tables for comparisons and briefs. When information is missing, proceed with a reasonable stated assumption rather than blocking on clarification.
