# Audience and intent

Identify who content serves and what they're trying to do. See `marin-digital-standards/content-design/principles.md` for the underlying audience/intent model this workflow applies.

## Core rule

An audience must be specific enough to guide decisions. "The public," "residents," or "users" is usually too broad unless segmented further.

## For each audience, define

Who they are · their relationship to the content · their task or decision · their context of use · their likely knowledge level · their urgency or emotional state · barriers they may face · what they need next.

Distinguish **primary** (whose task determines the content's structure — optimize for them first), **secondary** (also uses the content, but shouldn't distort the primary purpose — serve via links or related sections), and **internal** (staff/partners/decision-makers who need process or governance information — don't mix this into public-facing pages unless the public actually needs it).

## Intent categories

Classify into one dominant category — Do (complete an action: apply, pay, register, report), Know (understand something before deciding), Find (locate a person/service/place/document/status), Decide (choose among options), Comply (meet a requirement), Participate (attend, comment, volunteer, engage). See `marin-digital-standards/content-design/principles.md` for what each implies about content structure.

## Translate needs into user questions

Concrete, not abstract: "Am I eligible?" "What do I need before I start?" "How much does it cost?" "How long will it take?" "What happens after I submit?" "Who do I contact if something goes wrong?" "Is this still current?"

## Avoid organization-first framing

Weak: "We need a page about the department's program." Better: "Residents need to know whether they qualify, how to apply, and what happens after they apply."

## Quality checks

A good analysis names a specific audience segment, defines a task (not just a topic), separates public needs from internal needs, and identifies what the user already knows vs. needs next — with enough detail to shape title, structure, content type, and metadata.
