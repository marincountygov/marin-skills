# Inclusive language review

## Purpose

Support respectful, culturally competent, trauma-informed, disability-aware, age-aware, gender-aware, and resident-centered language.

**This skill is the review/rewrite workflow. It is not where the standard lives.** The category-by-category guidance — disability, gender/family/identity, trauma-informed language, age/income, race/ethnicity/national origin, public safety/health — is canonical in [marin-digital-standards/content-design/inclusive-language.md](https://github.com/marincountygov/marin-digital-standards/blob/main/content-design/inclusive-language.md), including each category's escalation triggers. Consult that document for *what the language should be*; use this skill for *how to review and rewrite a specific piece of content*.

## Standard workflow

1. **Identify the content purpose.** What must the resident understand or do? What decision, service, risk, or next step is involved?
2. **Identify the audience and context.** Who's affected? Are they seeking help, reporting harm, complying with a requirement, checking eligibility, responding to an emergency, or receiving a denial?
3. **Locate sensitive references.** Flag references to identity, disability, age, income, health, safety, housing, enforcement, immigration, family status, trauma, or legal status.
4. **Test necessity.** For each sensitive descriptor, ask whether it's needed for comprehension, service delivery, eligibility, safety, data accuracy, or legal compliance. Remove what isn't.
5. **Rewrite for respect and action**, per the relevant category in `marin-digital-standards/content-design/inclusive-language.md`. Keep the focus on services, choices, requirements, and next steps.
6. **Preserve required terms with explanation** — if technical, legal, medical, or program language must remain, define it in plain language near first use.
7. **Recommend escalation when needed** — see each category's escalation triggers in the standard. Route to legal counsel, privacy staff, HR, public health, emergency management, law enforcement leadership, equity staff, program owners, or communications leadership as appropriate; don't attempt the determination yourself.

## Output formats

**Reviewing content:**
- `Overall assessment` — generally respectful, risky, unclear, or likely to stigmatize.
- `Priority issues` — the most important language concerns.
- `Recommended rewrite` — revised text that keeps the original meaning.
- `Rationale` — brief explanation of the language choice.
- `Escalation needed` — any legal, privacy, policy, clinical, safety, or emergency review concerns.

**Creating content:**
- Audience and context assumptions.
- Draft language.
- Optional alternatives for formal, plain, urgent, or community-centered tone.
- Notes on terms that should be verified with the program owner or affected community.

## Do not

- Sanitize or weaken critical safety, eligibility, enforcement, or health information.
- Erase legally required terms.
- Assume identities, pronouns, diagnoses, immigration status, income status, family structure, or lived experience.
- Use euphemisms that obscure requirements, risks, or consequences.
- Make clinical, legal, HR, or civil-rights determinations — flag for the appropriate reviewer instead.
- Present an inclusive-language edit as a substitute for community review when content materially affects a specific community.
